How the plants are built

Leaf area from cover. Cover is converted to leaf area index with Beer's law, LAI = −ln(1 − cover) / k, with k = 0.5. Leaf area per plant is LAI × 286 cm², assuming 35 plants/m². That area is shared among the leaves present, weighted by leaf age and position.

Development from thermal time. Emergence at 90 °C·d, a new main-stem trifoliate every 55 °C·d (capped at 16), branches from nodes 2–5. Reproductive stages are assumed: R1 540, R3 720, R5 950, R6 1150, R7 1480, R8 1640 °C·d. All four systems share this phenology; differences come from your cover data.

Height. A logistic curve toward about 95 cm, adjusted by each system's leaf area relative to the four-system mean before canopy closure.

Senescence. After a system's peak cover, the fraction of peak LAI still present sets how many of the oldest leaves have dropped; leaves next in line turn yellow. Fallen leaves appear on the soil.

Top view. Each tile is 1.52 m of row by 0.76 m (one row width). Leaves are added from the row outward until the tile's green area matches the measured cover, so the tile shows your number exactly.

Residue. Surface residue levels are illustrative (plow ~5%, chisel ~30%, strip-till ~50% with a cleared row strip, no-till ~75%), not measured values.
