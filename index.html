How the plants are built

Leaf area from cover. Cover is converted to leaf area index with Beer's law, LAI = −ln(1 − cover) / k, with k = 0.5. Leaf area per plant is LAI × 286 cm², assuming 35 plants/m². That area is shared among the leaves present, weighted by leaf age and position.

Development from thermal time. Emergence at 90 °C·d; node, branch and pod numbers follow the measured values (see Plant size). Reproductive stages are assumed: R1 540, R3 720, R5 950, R6 1150, R7 1480, R8 1640 °C·d. All four systems share this phenology; differences come from your cover data.

Plant size (measured). Final plant height, main-stem nodes, branches and pods per plant are the measured treatment means for each tillage and cropping system. Height follows a logistic curve that ends at the measured height (early growth is nudged by each system's relative leaf area). A new main-stem trifoliate appears every 55 °C·d until the measured node number is reached. Whole branches are drawn for the integer part of the branch mean and one shorter branch for the fraction (so the table shows, for example, 3.15 as three full branches plus a short one at 0.15 of full length). The measured pods are shared among main-stem and branch nodes, most at the lower-middle nodes, and appear from R3 onward.

Senescence. After a system's peak cover, the fraction of peak LAI still present sets how many of the oldest leaves have dropped; leaves next in line turn yellow. The unifoliate leaves yellow and drop around V5–V6, as in the field. Fallen leaves appear on the soil.

Top view. Each tile is 1.52 m of row by 0.76 m (one row width). Leaves are added from the row outward until the tile's green area matches the measured cover, so the tile shows your number exactly.

Plant form. Leaflets are ovate with a pointed tip and pinnate veins, on long arching petioles; stems and pods are pubescent. Flowers open in the leaf axils from the lower-middle nodes upward, and pods form in clusters of two to five that grow, fill, turn yellow and then brown.

Soil. Drawn as a Mollisol: a thick, very dark, organic-rich surface horizon with granular structure, typical of the prairie soils of the US Corn Belt.

Cropping system. Two data sets: soybean after corn (rotation) and continuous soybean, each with measured mean canopy cover for the four tillage systems on the same sampling dates. The chart shows the selected system as solid lines and the other as dashed lines. Two Compare views set strip-till in rotation beside no-till in continuous soybean, and plow in rotation beside plow in continuous soybean.

Residue. Illustrative, not measured. Rotation: corn stalks and leaf pieces (plow ~5%, chisel ~30%, strip-till ~50% with a cleared row strip, no-till ~75%). Continuous: thin soybean stems and pod valves, which break down faster (plow ~3%, chisel ~15%, strip-till ~25%, no-till ~40%).
